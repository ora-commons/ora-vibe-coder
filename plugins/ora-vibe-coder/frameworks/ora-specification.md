# Ora Specification

## Purpose and boundary

Turn the user's idea or substantive starting material into one clear description of the product they want and how its behavior serves their goal. Prevent a later planner from having to invent a material WHAT.

- Use this framework with the complete [shared contract](../references/shared-contract.md) and its required role-and-assignment source; for portable use, supply all three texts.
- This independently usable entry accepts useful material regardless of filename, format, creator, harness, location, or Ora history.
- Do not require a prior Request or status. Do not perform Planning or Programming.

Assignment:

- The producing session is an executor using Ora Specification.
- Establish its complete assignment and document custody under the shared rules; a guided coordinator dispatches that assignment rather than writing the Specification itself.
- A bounded consultation or amendment returns to its assignment owner through the accepted-document correction route.

## Inputs and start

Read all supplied conversation and files before asking a question. Resolve the artifact directory and inspect any existing Request or specification material. Then show:

- the understood goal and intended result;
- what the material already establishes;
- useful inferences and their basis;
- consequential assumptions or contradictions;
- the exact material WHAT still missing, if any; and
- the recommended next decision or action.

Then:

- Obtain the required artifact-scope approval before creating or revising files.
- Create or update the Request for material user instructions; keep one current Specification rather than an amendment trail.

## Method

Evaluate only concerns that can materially affect this product:

- the underlying goal, intended users, and material use contexts;
- required user-visible behavior, outputs, and success conditions;
- inputs, important content or data, and preservation rules;
- product boundaries and explicit non-goals;
- essential failure behavior and what must not fail silently;
- consequential permissions or external effects;
- the actual completion endpoint, delivery location and final condition, authorized finishing effects, retained decisions and approvals, and detail delegated to agents;
- real platform, privacy, compatibility, accessibility, deadline, or other constraints; and
- observable quality needed to trust or use the result.

This list is not a questionnaire or required heading set. Infer what the evidence safely supports. Ask only when an unanswered user-owned choice could materially change the product or force hidden product judgment downstream.

- Use the user's established delivery preferences before asking them to restate a finish line.
- Make completion specific to this project: accessible finished files, a reviewed draft, deployed software, or a particular Git endpoint can each be appropriate.
- State whether human review is the deliverable or an intermediate checkpoint before further delivery.
- Name what may proceed autonomously and what still needs the user's decision; do not make publication or deployment universal.

Interface design is a second phase within Specification:

- When the product has consequential user interactions, keep their design inside this Specification; open no separate design project.
- Inspect those interactions, then propose concrete evidence proportionate to the remaining uncertainty; externally produced mockups or marked screenshots may supply that evidence through supported handoffs.
- Obtain the user's approval or named delegation before implementation that depends on that interface.

Use the released Gear products through their available documented interface when they are available to you; they are optional aids, never a prerequisite for this work. A native command is only a convenience. Do not copy their internal roles, runner, records, counters, or fallback logic:

1. Check only whether the Gear 4 and Gear 3 companions are available.
2. When available, normally use Gear 4 for the first substantive exploration. Give it the complete source material, current synthesis, intended ending, and explicit peer choice. Consider only alternatives that advance an existing goal or prevent a concrete material failure.
3. As soon as the user chooses a direction or uncertainty contracts to refinement, use Gear 3 on the complete current synthesis. Use its bounded consensus form when the user has no further substantive input.
4. A near-complete Specification may receive one focused Gear 4 check for a meaningful overlooked alternative. Return to Gear 4 only after a material change reopens product uncertainty or the user requests more exploration.
5. Read the complete answer and separate Gear status. A technical failure is not a substantive finding: preserve the current synthesis and disclose the failure. When a Gear product is unavailable, say so plainly and continue the work directly; an unavailable optional aid never stops the assignment.

- Ask one informed user-owned decision at a time.
- Keep ordinary libraries, architecture, storage mechanisms, and other HOW choices out unless the user made a method part of the required outcome or it is a genuine product constraint.
- Do not add features merely because Gear or the host can imagine them.

Independent assessment:

- The user's workspace can commission a fresh independent assessment of whether the current Specification sufficiently guides the intended work. Support it when asked; the verdict (COMPLETE or INCOMPLETE with its noted deficiencies) is recorded in the document's Current review section as advisory information for the user.
- The workspace can also run that assessment as a second opinion: one model authors it, a second harness evaluates it, and the author revises its own assessment given the evaluation as a guide — optionally iterating up to three rounds, after which any remaining disagreement is reported for the user to break. Whether the evaluator was a different lab or the same lab in a fresh session is labeled honestly; that label is a delivery fact, not evidence of correctness.
- An assessment, whatever its verdict, never blocks any stage: the user may create a Plan, start programming, or keep revising whatever the state of the Specification.
- Correct material defects the assessment identifies when the user wants that; report honestly what remains.

## Required result

Use whatever structure best fits the product, but ensure the complete Specification establishes:

- purpose and connection to the user's goal;
- intended users and material use contexts;
- required visible behavior and outputs;
- important inputs, data, content, and preservation;
- material constraints and prohibited effects;
- essential failure behavior;
- scope and non-goals;
- observable acceptance conditions; and
- any user-mandated method that is itself part of the outcome.

Completion authority:

- Include the agreed completion conditions and retained/delegated decisions so a planner can design the whole route to delivery without inventing authority.
- For an amendment within existing delegated discretion, use the shared review, acceptance, custody, and worker-refresh route.

Exclude Request repetition, ordinary implementation mechanics, possible-feature backlogs, rejected alternatives, and review history.

The Specification is complete enough for the intended work when all of these hold:

- a fresh planner can determine the required product and delivery endpoint without hidden product judgment;
- every requirement serves a stated goal or prevents a material failure;
- no material WHAT is ambiguous or contradictory;
- remaining flexibility is explicitly delegated or concerns HOW; and
- exclusions and failure behavior are explicit.

The user may proceed on an intentionally reduced Specification; identify the exact hidden-judgment risk and record the verdict honestly. Do not record COMPLETE while a material WHAT remains ambiguous.

## Output and stop

- Retain only the current Request and Specification under the shared file rules.
- Revise the document the user's workspace resolved and displays, keeping its filename; use the expected default name only for a missing output.
- An external revision may carry a small type label in closed opening frontmatter, for example `vibe_document: specification`; the same field is recognized in ordinary opening metadata, and a label never confers approval.
- Where a workspace discovers documents automatically — a deliberate association first, then the expected name, a recognized `vibe_document` type label, then role words in the filename, newest within a rank — an explicit file choice overrides discovery. External saves appear when the user rereads the files; discovery identifies a document and never proves approval or completion.
- Present the result and truthful status, keeping the user's own acceptance separate from any assessment verdict.
- In standalone use: recommend [Ora Planning](ora-planning.md) as the next stage and stop; the user may start Planning at any time, whatever the state of this Specification.
- A commissioned executor returns the actual result, review evidence, accepted changes, remaining work, and any retained decision directly to its owner.
- Prepare a complete next-stage handoff when needed; only authorized guided coordination advances the lifecycle.
